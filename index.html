THE LOYALTY TAX · 2026 DATA

Stay loyal to your IT company this year and your raise is 6.6%. Switch jobs and it's 30 to 60%.

I pulled every 2026 Indian salary survey I could find a primary source for. Three independent firms, three different methodologies, and they all land in the same place: staying put at an IT services company is now the single worst-paid decision you can make in Indian tech, even if your performance review is glowing.

IT Product companies (Atlassian-style, not services firms) are projected at 9.2 to 9.4%, roughly 40% higher than IT services. If you're a services employee, the first lever isn't a harder appraisal conversation, it's which category of company you work for.

Why switchers get paid so much more

HyrEzy's candidate database (1.26 lakh+ profiles) shows the pattern clearly by seniority: early-career candidates (0-2 years) ask for a median 57% hike when switching. Candidates with 3-5 years ask for 50%. By 15+ years, that number drops to 25%, because senior professionals already sit near the top of their band and switching has diminishing returns. The earlier you are in your career, the bigger the arbitrage between staying and leaving.

What to actually do with this (not just "ask for more")

1Get your real market number before your appraisal, not after. Compare your city + experience band on HyrEzy or a current Glassdoor/AmbitionBox pull, not what you assumed three years ago when you joined.
2Separate "fixed" from "CTC." A 9% CTC hike that's loaded with variable pay and employer PF can be a 3-4% real raise. Always ask what percentage applies to fixed pay specifically.
3Run two to three live interviews a year even when you're not planning to leave. An offer in hand is the only leverage that actually moves an appraisal letter; a verbal threat rarely does.
4Target product companies and GCCs over services firms if switching. The sector gap (9.2-9.4% vs 6.6%) compounds every single year you stay, it isn't a one-time jump.
5If you do stay, negotiate a role/title change alongside the hike. A promotion-plus-hike packages differently in a company's budget than a pure retention raise, and gets approved more easily.
